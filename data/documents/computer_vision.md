What Is Computer Vision

Computer vision is a field of AI that enables computers to interpret and
understand visual information, such as images and video, simulating the
way human vision works. The goal is to give machines the ability to "see"
and extract meaningful insight from visual data, not just store it as raw
pixels.

How Computer Vision Works

At a technical level, an image is just a grid of pixels, and each pixel is
represented by one or more numbers (a single grayscale value, or a
combination like RGB for color). Neural networks built for computer
vision process an image through many layers: early layers detect simple
features like edges, and later layers combine those features into more
complex shapes and, eventually, recognizable objects. Algorithms and deep
learning models trained this way can detect patterns, recognize objects,
and extract insight from images or video, such as identifying faces in a
photo or spotting obstacles for a self-driving car.

Real-World Applications

Computer vision powers a wide range of applications: facial recognition,
medical image analysis (for example, helping radiologists spot potential
tumors in X-rays or MRIs), autonomous driving, augmented reality,
agricultural drones that monitor crop health across large fields, and
automotive quality-control systems that inspect thousands of parts per
hour to catch defects smaller than a millimeter.
